# Purchase conviction research protocol

Research only. Preserve every existing public entry, score, outcome, grading rule, and live-model configuration. The previous experiments tested source aggregates and disclosure-day counts; this experiment explicitly tests economic size and participant behavior.

Build public-trade features from explicit filing dates, withholding same-day filings without publication times. Deduplicate repeated vendor/event records before aggregation. Congress amounts are ranges: use a conservative net lower bound (buy lower bounds minus sell upper bounds), report missing/unbounded ranges, and do not treat upper bounds as exact dollars. Insider dollar amounts should use disclosed shares times transaction price; compare against the same person's prior buying and, where supported, pre-purchase holdings. Treat missing amounts/holdings as unknown. Separate buyers, sells, grants, exercises, and scheduled-plan indicators rather than interpreting all insider activity as conviction.

Hypotheses: (1) uncapped log-dollar buy/sell volumes and conservative net demand outperform count-only direction; (2) unusually large insider purchases, increases in ownership, executive participation, and independent buyer clusters add predictive information; (3) Congress member weighting based only on previously matured, disclosure-following outcomes helps; (4) these signals qualify bullish forecasts better when combined with prices and market context. A targeted source cohort is distinct from the full ledger: always report coverage and compare the same eligible events.

Reuse the fixed chronological historical training and validation partitions. Select models on validation only, retaining at least 50% of bullish opportunities. The previously inspected historical test and actual ledger are exploratory confirmation checks, not untouched tests; do not claim an independently validated 75% rate from them. Test source-only and augmented feature sets, regularized logistic and constrained gradient-boosting models, a bullish qualification gate that preserves original bearish calls, and a ranking gate retaining at least half of eligible bullish opportunities. Evaluate 30D first, 7D separately. Report failures and confidence/sample-size limitations. Sparse subgroup success cannot justify changing production.
